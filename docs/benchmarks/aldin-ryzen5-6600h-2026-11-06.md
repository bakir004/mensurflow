## Stability

Output of `awk -f docs/benchmarks/compare_runs.awk build-bench-aldin/results/run1.txt build-bench-aldin/results/run2.txt`:

```text
group            rows      >10%      >25%  run2 slower  run2 faster
parallel t=12     198       112        68           67           45
parallel t=2      198        25         4           19            6
parallel t=4      198        37        10           27           10
parallel t=6      198        54        30           32           22
parallel t=8      198        13         3           11            2
sequential        198        31         7           20           11
(72 rows under 1 microsecond skipped)
```

The 12-thread rows are particularly noisy: 112/198 (56.6%) differ by more than 10%, including
68 by more than 25%. Eight threads were the most repeatable parallel setting, with 13/198 (6.6%)
over 10%; sequential still has 31/198 (15.7%). These runs are provisional, especially at 12 threads,
and do not support choosing a fixed thread policy. The cause was not isolated; affinity was unset.

Severe slowdowns are retained rather than discarded: at n = 100,000, `add` with 12 threads changes
from 0.24x to 3.59x, while `sigmoid` changes from 5.24x to 0.07x. Those cells require targeted repeat
measurements before use. Passing correctness checks does not make their timing stable.

## Observations

At n = 10,000,000, the best measured gains for simple arithmetic, sign/activation operations, and
`bias_add` are only about 1.08-1.35x. This is consistent with data movement limiting throughput,
but no hardware counters were collected to establish the bottleneck.

Math-heavy kernels benefit more: `exp` is fastest at 8 threads in both runs (4.31x / 4.08x),
`sigmoid` reaches 4.39x / 4.79x at 12 threads, and AXPY's best gains are 3.24x / 3.21x at 6 / 8 threads.
These portable-build results include standard math-library costs and do not isolate hardware FMA.
Twelve threads are not uniformly fastest.

For matmul at n = 1024, sequential achieves 22.311 GFLOP/s, the one-thread blocked kernel 19.112,
and the best measured count, 6 threads, 80.075; 12 threads achieve 71.129. This is one matmul run,
so its repeatability was not measured.

The global elementwise parallel threshold remains 32,768 elements. Small rows at or below that
threshold use the serial fallback, even if the requested thread count is higher. These timings
compare existing kernels and do not establish new per-operation cutoffs.

## Problems

- The initial portable GCC build of `ea9723b` failed in the new packed GEMM code with
  `inlining failed in call to always_inline ... target specific option mismatch` for AVX intrinsics
  inside `store_row` lambdas. Commit `f46fd51` applies the existing AVX2/FMA target attribute
  to those four lambda call operators. The Release build then completed and all 19 tests passed,
  with `MENSURFLOW_NATIVE=OFF` throughout. No global AVX flags were added.
- The README/template and AWK summaries still assumed scalar rows and two speedup columns.
  The same commit updates them for current output while retaining historical-format support:
  rates come from column 7, absent scalar data is `n/a`, and the CPU sequential row represents
  the one-thread baseline. Separate blocked matmul one-thread measurements remain separate.
- Static review of the nine operation adapters found no issue: unary arguments match their kernels;
  `sqrt` uses nonnegative input; AXPY uses `alpha=1.25f` and `std::fma`; the compact bias feature count
  divides every input size. Timed parallel elementwise results passed bitwise checks against the
  one-thread result; independent operation semantics are covered by the passing CTest suite.
- RAM type/speed and per-stick details are unknown because this session cannot read SMBIOS tables.
