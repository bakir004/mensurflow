## What I ran

```
build-bench/softmax_loss_benchmark
build-bench/bandwidth_benchmark                     # the ceiling
python benchmarks/reference/softmax_loss.py         # PyTorch 2.15.0, NumPy 2.5.4, Python 3.14.7
```

PyTorch is timed on its own kernels with preallocated outputs where ATen allows it (`aten._softmax.out`,
`_log_softmax.out`, `_softmax_backward_data.out`, `_log_softmax_backward_data.out`,
`mse_loss_backward.grad_input`), `F.cross_entropy` / `F.mse_loss` /
`F.binary_cross_entropy_with_logits` for the losses, and for `cross_entropy_backward` the two kernels its
autograd runs (`nll_loss_backward`, then `_log_softmax_backward_data`). BCE backward has no single ATen
kernel, so its reference is the formula `(sigmoid(x) - t) / n` in torch ops. Attention is compared
with both `F.scaled_dot_product_attention` (fused on the CPU) and the same naive algorithm in torch ops.

## Ceiling

`bandwidth_benchmark`, a float32 copy of 64M floats: **100.7 GB/s on one thread, 123.0 GB/s on 11
threads**. The "% of copy ceiling" column divides our best rate by 123 GB/s. Every op here except
`mse_loss_backward` is far from it: they are compute-bound on `exp` (see below), not on memory. A row
above 100% fits in the caches, so the DRAM ceiling does not apply to it.

## Why we are behind where we are

- **`exp` is scalar.** Softmax, log-softmax and cross-entropy spend their time in one `std::exp` per
  element: about 1.8 ns per element on one thread (15.5 ms for 8.4M elements); with all threads they
  reach only 4-17% of the memory ceiling. Clang cannot vectorize `std::exp` (the libm call has no NEON version), while ATen
  uses a vectorized polynomial `exp` (`Vectorized<float>::exp`). That is why one-thread large shapes are
  at 0.5-0.65x of PyTorch while small shapes, where call overhead dominates, are at parity or faster.
  log_softmax is behind more often than softmax because PyTorch's log_softmax kernel is the more
  optimized of the two (it beats its own softmax by 1.1-2.8x on four of the five shapes; our two take the same time). Next step: a
  vectorizable `exp` (range reduction to 2^k * e^r and a degree-5 polynomial in plain C++ that the
  compiler turns into NEON or AVX2), used by softmax, log-softmax, cross-entropy and BCE.
- **`softmax_backward` is memory-bound and not yet at the ceiling**: 74% at 16384 x 512 (we read y and
  the gradient twice: once for the dot product, once for the output). PyTorch is at about 97%.
- **Attention**: the GEMMs on this Mac use the plain-loop fallback (no NEON micro-kernel), which
  makes one-thread attention 4x slower than PyTorch's naive version (its matmul runs on Apple
  Accelerate). With 11 threads the gap closes to 0.73-0.84x. The fused SDPA is 5-11x faster than
  either naive version because it never writes the [L, L] scores to memory (FlashAttention-style
  tiling); that is the P4 "fused attention" item, not this week's.

## Problems found and fixed

- **`mse_loss_backward` was 1.6-4x slower with threads at 65,536 elements** (0.0067 ms on one thread,
  0.0105-0.027 ms on 2-8). It is a memory-bound loop and used the 32,768 cutoff; it now uses the
  262,144 cutoff of the matrix and movement kernels, and runs at one-thread speed at that size. The
  reduced MSE forward and BCE keep 32,768: their per-element work (double conversion, `exp`) makes
  threads pay off there (1.8x at 65,536).
- **`log_softmax` lost about 3e-5 near logits of 1000** because `max + log(sum)` was rounded to float
  before subtracting; it now computes `(x - max) - log(sum)`, which is exact for the largest elements.
  Found by the stability test.
- **The cross-entropy and BCE backward kernels read `gradient[0]` with `Reduction::None`**, which crashed
  on an empty batch (no gradient values at all). They now read it only for Sum and Mean.
- **BCE's gradient `sigmoid(x) - t` cancelled** for confident predictions (x = 8, t = 1 lost three
  digits). It is now `(1 - t) - e/(1+e)` or `e/(1+e) - t` with e = exp(-|x|), exact in that case; at
  x = 30 it returns -9.36e-14 where PyTorch returns 0.
