## Summary

  their tile sizes are parameters that can be changed at run time.
- The portable CPU matmul (used without AVX2, for example on ARM) takes its tile sizes as a parameter
  too. The AVX2 packed kernel already had them (`PackedGemmBlocking`).
- Tests: a new `matmul_tile_test`, and the existing `matmul_test` now also runs on `metal:0`. All
  33 tests pass.
- Measured on the M3 Pro: [`docs/benchmarks/bakir-m3pro-2026-11-14.md`](../benchmarks/bakir-m3pro-2026-11-14.md).
- Removed the outdated `docs/SPECS.md` and `docs/TECHNOLOGY_OVERVIEW.md`.
- Auto-tuning, first mode (offline, tested): `mf::Algorithm` and `mf::Tuner` tune the matmul calls of a

## 1. Metal matmul (`src/backends/metal/gemm.{hpp,mm}`, registered in `backend.mm`)

- Three kernels, chosen by `MetalGemmConfig::kernel`:
  - 0 **tiled**: threadgroup tiles of bm x bn, bk-deep slices of A and B in threadgroup memory, tm x tn
    outputs per thread;
  - 1 **simdgroup** (the default): the same tiles, multiplied in 8 x 8 blocks with `simdgroup_matrix`
    by sg_m x sg_n simdgroups;
  - 2 **MPS** (`MPSMatrixMultiplication`), the reference.
- The shader is compiled once per tile configuration, with the sizes as preprocessor constants
  (threadgroup arrays need compile-time sizes), and the pipelines are cached. Slices are zero-padded
  past the matrix edges, so only the final store checks bounds; an 8 x 8 block that crosses the edge
  is stored through threadgroup memory.
- Inputs are read in place, with the view's offset passed to the encoder as a byte offset.
- `metal::set_metal_gemm_config` validates the configuration: divisibility, at most 1024 threads, at
  most 32 KB of threadgroup memory, and at most 64 accumulators per thread. On a GPU without
  `simdgroup_matrix` (older than Apple family 7), kernel 1 falls back to the tiled kernel.
- Like the existing MPSGraph ops, a call waits for the GPU before it returns. Strided views of Metal
  tensors are read straight from shared memory without a synchronize, so returning before the GPU
  finishes would not be safe yet.

## 2. CPU tile sizes (`src/backends/cpu/ops/blas/blas.{hpp,cpp}`)

- `MatmulBlocking { rows, depth, cols }`, with the old constants (whole band, 128, 256) as the
  defaults. There is an overload of `matmul_f32_parallel` that takes it per call, plus
  `matmul_blocking()` and `set_matmul_blocking()` for the default.
- `rows` is new: the band of a thread can be walked in smaller row blocks. Every C[i,j] still adds its
  products in increasing p, so every tile size gives the same bits. The test checks this for 6 tile
  sizes at 1, 3 and 11 threads.

## 3. Tests (`tests/ops/matmul_tile_test.cpp`)

- CPU: bit-identical results for every blocking and thread count, the setter, and its errors.
- Metal: 10 configurations (all three kernels, small to 128-wide tiles, a single 8 x 8 block) on 14
  shapes that do not divide by the tiles, plus views that start partway into their buffer; 9 invalid
  configurations rejected.

## 4. Results (M3 Pro)

- Default Metal matmul: 2.3 to 3.0 TFLOP/s from 1024^3 up, which is 0.76 to 0.89x MPS, and 1.1 to
  1.4x faster than MPS at 256^3 and 512^3. The tiled kernel reaches 1.5 TFLOP/s.
- What mattered: at most 8 accumulator blocks per simdgroup (16 is three times slower) and vector loads
  (+30%). Padding and unrolling made no difference.
- CPU: wider tiles (512 x 1024 or 4 x 64 x 1024) are up to 1.2x faster than the default at three of
  four shapes, so the best tile size depends on the shape.

**Problem found while measuring**: the GPU needs time under load to raise its clock, and a single
warm-up call is not enough. The benchmark now keeps the GPU busy for one second first; without that,
whichever configuration came first was measured at about a third of its speed.

## 5. Auto-tuning (`include/mensurflow/tuning.hpp`, `src/core/tuning.{hpp,cpp}`)

- Kernel settings are now passed per call: `OpArgs::tuning`, filled by `dispatch()` from the active
  tuner. Backends declare a kernel tunable with `Dispatcher::register_tunable` (name, version, key,
  parameter names, defaults, candidates). Matmul on Metal and on CPUs without AVX2 are tunable.
- `Tuner::tune(algorithm, inputs)` runs the algorithm once and records its tunable calls. Each call is
  measured with the defaults and with every candidate that agrees with the defaults' result, and the
  fastest is stored. Then the whole algorithm is timed with and without the results, which are dropped
  if it got slower. `Algorithm::run(inputs, tuner)` applies them.
- Results go to a plain text file, one line per call under a `machine` line, readable and editable;
  calls already stored are not measured again.
- `tests/core/tuning_test.cpp`: the file format (round trip, two machines, malformed lines with line
  numbers), tuning on the CPU and on Metal, reuse, reload, and a hand-written result that reaches the
  kernel. 34 of 34 tests pass.
- `examples/tune_mlp.cpp` (1024 -> 4096 -> 1024 -> 512, batch 256): 1.12x faster on Metal (MPS won the
  two large products) and 1.30x on the CPU (batch 128) after tuning; tuning took 15 s, and the second
  run reuses the file in 1.5 s.

## 6. Dashboard autotune and device choice

- Every op page has an **Autotune** section (`observe tune`, `POST /api/tune`): the input parameters a
  call is tuned for, the tuned settings against the defaults, every candidate's time as a chart, and the
  stored results.
- The tuner also chooses the **device** of every op called with `Execution::Auto`: each device is timed
  through dispatch with the copies there and back, after its own kernel settings were tuned, and must
  agree with the CPU's result. The result is stored as a `place` line and used by
  `Algorithm::run(inputs, tuner)`; without one, Auto stays on the CPU. On the M3 Pro with data on the
  CPU: a 512^3 matmul goes to the GPU (0.75 ms with copies vs 1.53 ms), a 65,536-element add stays on
  the CPU (31 us vs 280 us).

- **Matmul CPU-or-GPU map** on the matmul page: m × n × k from 2^1 to 2^12, refined level by level on the
  log scale (12, 23, 43 sizes per axis), measured in the background and drawn as it arrives, with a slider
  for k. Level 0 (1,728 shapes) takes under a minute. With the data on the CPU, the GPU wins only when m,
  n and k are all large (from about 256 to 512 up); everything else stays on the CPU because of the
  copies.

## 7. Tuning as minimizing a function (2026-11-15)

- A call's tuning is now the minimization of `time(arguments ; kernel parameters)`: the parameters
  (m, n, k, threads) are fixed by the call, the arguments are the kernel's settings, each with a few
  ordered discrete values. A kernel declares its space with `space(args)` (one region per kernel
  variant, each with its axes) and `allowed(args, config)`, replacing the fixed candidate list.
- Two search methods (`TuneOptions::search`):
  - **binary sampling** (the default): along one argument at a time from the default, sample the
    line coarse to fine (ends, middle, quarters), measure every sample and keep the best; repeat over
    the axes until nothing moves;
  - **exhaustive**: every allowed point. The search (`detail::search`) takes the objective as a function, so more methods
  can be added; a test checks both on a synthetic function with a known minimum.
- Binary sampling measured 34 of 1,114 points for a 1024^3 Metal matmul (about 4 s) and 21 of 80 for a
  512^3 CPU matmul (2 s, 1.37x).
- Dashboard: a search-method choice; per call the function being minimized, the fixed kernel
  parameters, the arguments with the values searched (argmin filled, default outlined), the lowest
  point of each variant, the search in the order points were measured with the best so far, and
  slices of the time along each argument through the minimum.
- **Inconsistent results, fixed.** The first version used a binary search along each argument.
  - In five identical runs, the CPU tune (512^3) chose different depths, and two runs ended at
    1.65 ms instead of 1.42.
  - Measuring every point twice showed the noise is small (median 0.4%), but depth 128, 256 and 512
    form a plateau (1.68, 1.66, 1.67 ms). The search's first comparison, 128 against 256, was
    decided by noise.
  - The binary search was therefore replaced by binary sampling, which measures the samples and keeps
    the best, so no comparison throws half the line away.
  - On Metal, the default's time varied with the GPU clock (0.85 to 1.21 ms). The default and the
    three fastest points are now timed again together at the end.
  - Now 5 of 5 runs agree: CPU depth 32, cols 512, 1.37 to 1.39x (rows is a tie); Metal MPS, 1.09
    to 1.16x.

## 8. Faster tuning: early stop (2026-11-16)

- The search now hands the objective a batch of points (all the samples of one line, or a region's
  grid); the choices are the same as before.
- **Early stop** (`TuneOptions::give_up`, 1.3, on by default): after 3 samples, a point whose fastest
  sample is already 1.3x the best so far stops being timed. A 1024^3 Metal tune's search went from 2.0
  to 2.7 s to 0.9 to 1.1 s, still choosing MPS; on the CPU (points close together) it saves little.
- Timing several points at once on separate threads was tried and removed: the search was 2 to 5 times
  faster, but under load the best tiles move (depth 64 or 128 instead of 32), and the results were
  1.01 to 1.18x instead of 1.36 to 1.45x.
- Dashboard: an *Early stop* control; per call the search's time and the points stopped early (hollow in
  the chart).
